A galactic rotation curve describes the orbital velocities of stars and gas as a function of distance from the galactic centre. It carries direct physical information about mass distribution and gravitational dynamics.

Each galaxy is treated on its own physical scale. Galactic radial coordinates are analysed in kiloparsecs, galaxy distances in megaparsecs and rotation velocities in kilometres per second.

r [kpc]    ·    D [Mpc]    ·    v(r) [km/s]

Consistent treatment of physical scales and units forms a fundamental part of galaxy modelling. Galaxy distance, radial coordinates, baryonic masses and characteristic velocities are processed within a common numerical framework.

The observed rotation curve of each galaxy is incorporated into the computational model. Contributions from gas, the stellar disk and the bulge, where present, are tracked separately.

vbar2 = vgas2 + vdisk2 + vbulge2

The theoretical galactic solution is calculated on the same radial grid. Predicted velocity profiles can therefore be compared directly with observed rotation curves.

The Baryonic Tully–Fisher Relation represents a fundamental scaling relation across galaxy populations. Computational BTFR analysis therefore requires galaxies in the data set to be studied within a common physical plane.

Total baryonic mass and characteristic outer rotation velocity are determined for each galaxy. These quantities are placed on a common BTFR plane to study population behaviour.

Mb   ↔   Vflat     Mb ∝ Vflat4

Each point on the BTFR diagram represents an individual galaxy. Rotation-curve analysis can therefore be connected with a population-level test.

Baryonic masses, flat velocities, observational uncertainties and theoretical predictions are processed within a common numerical data structure. Results are visualised on linear and logarithmic axes.

Galactic systems extend across a broad range of physical scales. Their dynamical structure from the central region to the outer disk is studied through kiloparsec radial scales and kilometre-per-second velocity profiles.

Our Galaxy Dynamics Simulations model the baryonic structure and rotational dynamics of galaxies numerically. Gas distributions, stellar disks, bulge components, baryonic masses, radial scales and orbital velocities are followed within a common physical system.

Each simulation preserves the physical scale of the galaxy. Galaxy distances are represented in Mpc, galactic radial coordinates in kpc and velocity profiles in km/s.

D [Mpc]   →   r [kpc]   →   v(r) [km/s]

Numerical solutions are followed radially from the central region to the outer rotation curve. The baryonic inner structure, transition regime and outer galactic velocity behaviour can therefore be examined within the same simulation.

GALACTIC STRUCTURE

Gas, stellar disk, bulge and total baryonic structure are incorporated into the numerical model.

RADIAL DYNAMICS

Radial velocity profiles are calculated from the galactic centre toward the outer regions.

ROTATION CURVES

Simulated rotation curves are compared with observed velocity measurements.

GALAXY COMPARISON

Galaxies with different masses, dimensions and surface densities are analysed within a common computational framework.

Can the same galactic-dynamics framework numerically reproduce the observed rotational behaviour of galaxies across different physical scales?

Simulation outputs are visualised through rotation curves, radial velocity profiles, baryonic-component plots and galaxy-to-galaxy comparisons.

BTFR analysis and galaxy-dynamics simulations form complementary parts of the same computational research programme. Individual galaxies are studied through their dynamical simulations, while the full galaxy sample is compared through baryonic mass and characteristic velocity scaling.
